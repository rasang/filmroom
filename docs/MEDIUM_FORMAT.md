# 120 output frame contract

This feature changes the final crop and film-edge presentation only. It does not change the native scientific `filmFormatMm`, grain, halation or optical computation. The film-width scientific control remains independently adjustable. It is not a claim of complete camera/120 optical simulation.

## Available frames

Nine physical stocks support 120: Kodak Portra 160, 400, 800, Gold 200, Ektar 100, Ektachrome E100, Fujifilm Provia 100F, Velvia 100 and historical Pro 400H. Portra 800 +1/+2 are processing aliases of the same physical 800 artwork and edge strip. No 120 option is fabricated for the other profiles. Choosing another stock without a 120 version returns to its existing frame. The 135 path, existing monochrome frames and immutable historical queue records retain their earlier contracts.

The output format is selected directly inside Composition, alongside the existing landscape/portrait controls. Format, orientation and crop form one draft: Cancel restores the whole prior composition, and Confirm creates one undoable edit. A nullable medium-format setting represents the existing 135 output. In 120 mode, perforation-matte selection is disabled because 120 has no perforations. Landscape/portrait is explicit, including square 6×6, where orientation selects which pair of sides carries the long film edges.

Nominal design geometry is 61 mm strip width, 56 mm image width across the strip and longitudinal apertures of 41.5, 56, 70, 84 and 168 mm for 645, 6×6, 6×7, 6×9 and 6×17. Each end adds a 3 mm design margin. These are nominal model dimensions, not measurements of a particular camera gate; real camera apertures and scanned margins differ. 645 landscape rotates the strip so that its rails are left/right. Other landscape apertures use top/bottom rails. One uniform scale is used, without stretching text or reshuffling it per frame size.

## Evidence and unresolved detail

The edges are synthesized reference reconstructions, not sampled scans. Each physical stock uses one finite continuous master strip and all five apertures sample it at the same frozen strip center. This proves continuity of the authored artwork, not exact correspondence to a physical roll. Typography, absolute spacing, inter-edge phase and color remain approximate. Unknown markings are omitted, not invented or implied to be physically blank. No light leaks are added. No third-party photograph pixels are shipped.

- Portra 160: a photographed 120 roll shows 41–55 and the other side 1–12 with intermediate solid right triangles. The original numbering and spacing are retained, with flatter lower arrows guided by the same-stock reference; 56 and absolute physical phase are not newly verified. [Reference](https://www.photrio.com/forum/attachments/img_e4614-jpg.283384/).
- Portra 400: one continuous photographed strip now supports upper 48–52, lower 7–9 and intermediate solid right triangles, including the area missing from the earlier limited illustration. A local nominal-aperture calibration supports the full 6×17 window; no unseen markings are extended past its evidence boundary. [FLOS Stories 3, page 38](https://www.light11.de/download/Flos_Stories_Ausgabe_3.pdf#page=38).
- Portra 800: photographed contact sheets support the 800-2 variant, the upper sequence and lower 1–12 with intermediate solid right triangles. The batch suffix and absolute spacing are not generalized to every roll. [State Film Lab](https://statefilm.com/digital-contact-sheets/) and [independent Sam Muller contact sheet](https://www.monsterchildren.com/articles/andrew-peters-20-year-issue).
- Gold 200: one photographer-identified Gold 200/Mamiya 645 darkroom contact print supports upper 41–45/KODAK 200 and lower 1–4 with intermediate arrows. The dim lower 1 has approximate typography; photographed gaps are not assumed to be universally uniform. [Source and identification](https://www.photrio.com/forum/threads/lucky-c200-vs-kodak-gold-200-in-120-side-by-side-comparison.217701/).
- Ektar 100: one continuous three-square strip supports upper 48–51/KODAK EKTAR 100 and lower 7–9 plus intermediate arrows. It replaces the earlier reconstruction from separate short references. Source resolution limits exact font strokes and color. [Murray Eskdale catalogue, page 5](https://rdsgallery.co.nz/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/2025_Eskdale_catalogue_sm.pdf#page=5).
- E100: one continuous three-square strip from Laura Cogan's E100/Hasselblad roll supports upper 48–51/KODAK E100 and lower 7–9 with arrows. No other strip or roll is stitched into it. Conservative local nominal-aperture calibration still exceeds the 174 mm presentation window, but perspective and curvature limit landmark accuracy. E100 is E-6 reversal film, not a C-41 negative. [Whole-roll account](https://www.35mmc.com/23/04/2021/the-whole-roll-and-nothing-but-the-roll-kodak-e100-in-iceland-a-journey-into-colour-by-laura-cogan/).
- Provia 100F: this illustration follows the official 120 diagram, including two marked edges and outlined right arrows with short shafts. A separate actual Safelight strip shows a single printed edge with solid left triangles, so the diagram layout is not a universal physical-roll claim. No hybrid layout or invented production code is used. [Data sheet, page 4](https://asset.fujifilm.com/www/us/files/2025-06/76ccbf624f1b5db023c093c94917708f/films_provia-100f_datasheet_01.pdf#page=4), [physical variant](https://safelightberlin.com/products/120-color-slide-film-developing).
- Velvia 100: the official 120 diagram explicitly places FUJI before 2 and 4, with a terminal 19. and solid left triangles. Only those locally confirmed omissions are corrected; no universal every-even-number brand cycle is inferred. The opposite blank edge is the diagram's portrayal, not proof for every batch. [Data sheet, page 6](https://asset.fujifilm.com/master/emea/files/2020-10/2f3c7f90a0b0c6e605e84f98b7d489c2/films_velvia-100_datasheet_01.pdf#page=6).
- Pro 400H: the official diagram supports FUJI before 2 and 19 with terminal 19.; a separate physical strip supports FUJI 6. and 8. Those instances are included without inventing a brand-repeat cycle or production numbers. The opposite blank edge remains diagram-based. [Data sheet, page 6](https://www.fujifilm.com.hk/products/professional_films/pdf/pro_400h_datasheet.pdf#page=6), [actual strip](https://safelightberlin.com/cdn/shop/products/120-color-film-developing-116907.jpg?v=1646324668).

Per-revision calibration, source images' public URLs, coverage bounds and procedural generators are recorded in `app/src/main/assets/medium120/v2/README.txt` and `tools/medium120/`. The four locally rebuilt Kodak spans cover all five default windows. Fuji's inherited full-length atlas bounds describe authored geometry, not a physically measured continuous roll.

These source audit notes retain the evidence and limits; the editor does not display a separate 120 reference notice. Positive/reversal edge colors are authored display colors. Any route-specific negative rendering is a synthetic presentation conversion, not a measured stock transmission model. Source URLs document the evidence; they do not grant permission to redistribute reference photos.

## Reviewed lower-rail revisions

The current set uses Portra 160/400/800 v5, Gold 200/Ektar 100/E100 v4, and the three Fuji v2 illustrations. Their exact version IDs, encoded byte counts and hashes are registered in `MediumStripDefinitions.kt`. All 24 historical and current PNGs remain available; obsolete candidates are never substituted for a frozen snapshot.

The v4 compiler places reusable authored arrow and digit atoms using a separate coordinate model for each stock. Portra 400, Ektar 100 and E100 use independently fitted three-arrow patterns within their observed finite spans. Gold 200 uses its finite observed sequence. Portra 800 uses an approximate midpoint pattern; its absolute pitch and phase are authored, not factory measurements. A model/placement guard rejects drift, and the upper rails are unchanged.

The final v5 revision flattens only Portra 160/400/800 arrows vertically, with stock-specific reference guidance. The visible horizontal boundaries, vertical centers, number positions and spacing remain fixed. At the documented 45% contrast threshold the authored arrow boxes change from 7×8 to 7×4, 16×11 to 16×9, and 12×13 to 12×7 atlas pixels. Weak antialias fringes are not represented by that threshold. All changes are restricted to the declared arrow boxes; no other stock, upper-rail or digit pixels change.

Portable scripts, source URLs, uncertainty and exact before/after manifests are in `tools/medium120/v4/` and `tools/medium120/v5/`. They consume only existing authored assets and numeric data. Review photographs and sample compositions are not bundled.

## Persistence and compatibility

The editor persists format and explicit orientation independently of science. Frame snapshots use geometry version 5, pin the physical asset version and strip center, and freeze their source/canonical sizes, crop and appearance. Queue container version 6 prevents older apps from interpreting a medium-format job as 135. Asset lookup and caching use the exact physical stock plus asset version. A new illustration is appended under a new path; previously pinned v1 and later revisions retain their original bytes and centers for queued exports. New captures use the latest registered revision. The complete presentation window, including both end allowances, must fit that revision's documented coverage. Preview, final exports, interactive overlays and queue scan thumbnails must all use the same versioned geometry and raster. Changing format, stock, orientation or undo history must not alter an already-enqueued job.
